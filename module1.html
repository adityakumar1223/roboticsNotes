🤖 Robotic Automation Introduction

Basics of automation, industrial robot use, control systems, robot anatomy, DOF, actuators and manipulators.

📌 Core Definitions

Robotics: The branch of engineering and science that involves the conception, design, manufacture, and operation of robots. It combines mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, and computer science.
Automation: The technique of making an apparatus, process, or system operate automatically with minimal human intervention. It improves efficiency, consistency, and safety.
Robot: A machine that is programmed to carry out complex tasks automatically. A robot can sense its environment, process information, and take actions.
Robot Anatomy: The physical structure of a robot — including its body (links), joints, end-effector, actuators, sensors, and controller — that together enable movement and task execution.
Degrees of Freedom (DOF): The number of independent movements a robot can perform. Each joint adds one DOF. A standard industrial robot arm has 6 DOF for full spatial movement.
Robot Manipulator: The arm-like mechanical structure of a robot consisting of links and joints that mimics the motion of a human arm to perform tasks.
Actuator: A device that converts energy (electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic) into mechanical motion. Examples: DC motors, servo motors, stepper motors, pneumatic cylinders.
Torque: A rotational force that causes an object to rotate around an axis. In robots, torque = Force × Distance from pivot. Measured in Newton-meters (Nm).
Pitch, Yaw, Roll: Three rotational motions — Pitch (up/down tilt), Yaw (left/right rotation), Roll (side-to-side tilt). Together they describe full 3D orientation.

📋 Three Laws of Robotics (Isaac Asimov)

  1. First Law: A robot may not injure a human being, or through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm.
  2. Second Law: A robot must obey the orders given by human beings except where such orders would conflict with the First Law.
  3. Third Law: A robot must protect its own existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Law.
💡 Exam Tip: Remember the priority order — Human safety > Obeying humans > Self-preservation.

🔁 Control Systems in Robotics

Open Loop Control System: No feedback. The controller sends a command and doesn't check if the output was correct. Simple but not accurate.
Example: A washing machine timer — runs for set time regardless of cleanliness.
Closed Loop Control System: Uses feedback from sensors to continuously correct output. More accurate and reliable.
Example: A thermostat — measures temperature and adjusts heating accordingly.
Fig 1: Open Loop (top) vs Closed Loop Control System (bottom)
Servo Control System: A closed-loop system that uses a servo motor and feedback (usually an encoder) to precisely control position, speed, or torque. Used in: Robot arms, CNC machines, camera systems.
Non-Servo Control System: An open-loop system where the robot follows a fixed path or set of commands with no feedback. Cheaper but less precise. Used in: Simple pick-and-place machines, conveyors.

✅ Characteristics of a Good Control System

  • Accuracy – Output matches desired input precisely
  • Stability – System returns to equilibrium after disturbance
  • Speed – Quick response to changes in input
  • Sensitivity – Responds appropriately to small input changes
  • Reliability – Consistent performance over time
  • Robustness – Works correctly despite external disturbances

❓ Short Type Questions & Answers

Short  Define the term Robotics.

Robotics is the branch of technology that deals with the design, construction, operation, and use of robots. It combines mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, and computer science to create machines that can perform tasks automatically or semi-automatically.

Key aspects: Perception (sensing), Processing (computing), Action (movement).

Short  Write down the three laws of Robot.

Isaac Asimov's Three Laws of Robotics:

  1. First Law: A robot may not injure a human being, or through inaction, allow a human to be harmed.
  2. Second Law: A robot must obey human orders unless they conflict with the First Law.
  3. Third Law: A robot must protect its own existence unless that conflicts with the First or Second Law.
Short  What is Robot Anatomy?

Robot Anatomy refers to the physical structure of a robot. It includes:

  • Links – Rigid body segments
  • Joints – Connectors allowing movement
  • Actuators – Motors that create motion
  • End-Effector – Gripper or tool at the tip
  • Sensors – Devices for perception
  • Controller – Brain of the robot
Short  Define DOF in the context of Robotics.

DOF (Degrees of Freedom) is the number of independent parameters that define the configuration of a robot. Each joint adds one DOF.

  • A robot with 6 DOF can reach any position and orientation in 3D space.
  • More DOF = more flexibility but more complexity.
  • Example: Human arm has 7 DOF (3 shoulder + 1 elbow + 3 wrist).
Short  Define Pitch, Yaw and Roll.
  • Pitch: Rotation about the lateral (side-to-side) axis — nodding motion (up/down tilt).
  • Yaw: Rotation about the vertical axis — turning left or right.
  • Roll: Rotation about the longitudinal (front-back) axis — tilting sideways.

Together, pitch, yaw, and roll describe the complete orientation of a robot or its end-effector in 3D space.

Short  What are actuators?

Actuators are output devices that convert energy into mechanical motion. They are the "muscles" of a robot.

  • Electric actuators: DC motors, Servo motors, Stepper motors
  • Pneumatic actuators: Use compressed air (fast but less precise)
  • Hydraulic actuators: Use pressurized fluid (very powerful)
Short  What is a robot Manipulator?

A robot manipulator is the arm-like structure of a robot that consists of a series of links connected by joints. It mimics the human arm and is designed to move objects, perform tasks, or interact with the environment.

Key parts: Base → Links → Joints → Wrist → End-Effector

Short  Give examples of open loop and closed loop control systems.

Open Loop Examples:

  • Washing machine (timer-based)
  • Traffic signal (fixed timing)
  • Electric toaster

Closed Loop Examples:

  • Thermostat (temperature feedback)
  • Cruise control in cars
  • Servo motor with encoder
  • Robot arm with position sensor
Short  Define Torque, Moment of Inertia, and Power.
  • Torque (τ): Rotational force = Force × Perpendicular distance. Unit: Nm. It is what makes a robot joint rotate.
  • Moment of Inertia (I): Resistance of a body to rotational acceleration. Depends on mass distribution. Unit: kg·m². Higher I = more torque needed.
  • Power (P): Rate of doing work = Force × Velocity (linear) or Torque × Angular velocity. Unit: Watts (W).

Long  Explain the Servo Control System with suitable diagram.

A Servo Control System is a closed-loop feedback system that controls the position, speed, or torque of a mechanical system (servo motor) very precisely.

Components:

  • Reference Input (Setpoint): Desired position or speed command
  • Error Detector: Compares desired vs actual output
  • Controller (Amplifier): Processes the error signal
  • Servo Motor: Actuator that produces motion
  • Feedback Sensor (Encoder/Tachometer): Measures actual output

Working Principle:

  1. Desired position (setpoint) is given as input.
  2. Error = Setpoint – Actual output
  3. Controller amplifies error signal and drives the motor.
  4. Motor moves the robot joint.
  5. Encoder feeds back actual position to the comparator.
  6. Process repeats until error = 0.

Applications: Robot arm joints, CNC machines, throttle control, camera gimbals.

💡 Key Advantage: Servo systems are self-correcting — they continuously adjust to maintain accuracy.

Long  Define DOF and explain its significance in robotics.

DOF (Degrees of Freedom) is the number of independent variables (parameters) needed to completely define the configuration or state of a mechanical system.

In Robotics: Each joint in a robot provides one DOF. The total DOF = number of joints (for simple serial robots).

Significance:

  • Workspace Coverage: More DOF = larger and more complex workspace the robot can cover.
  • Dexterity: 6 DOF allows full 3D positioning (x,y,z) and orientation (pitch, yaw, roll).
  • Obstacle Avoidance: Extra DOF (redundant robots, 7+ DOF) allow path planning around obstacles.
  • Task Complexity: Simple tasks need fewer DOF (2–3), while surgery or assembly needs 6+.

Types of DOF:

  • Translational DOF: Movement in x, y, z directions (linear)
  • Rotational DOF: Rotation about axes (pitch, yaw, roll)
💡 Example: Human arm has 7 DOF (3 shoulder + 1 elbow + 3 wrist), which is why we can reach any point in space while keeping our hand oriented correctly.
Long  Explain the revolute and prismatic joints with examples.

1. Revolute Joint (Rotary Joint):

  • Allows rotational motion about a fixed axis (like a hinge).
  • Denoted by R in robot notation.
  • Provides 1 DOF.
  • Examples: Elbow joint in robot arm, door hinge, human knee joint.

2. Prismatic Joint (Sliding Joint):

  • Allows linear/translational motion along an axis (like a piston).
  • Denoted by P in robot notation.
  • Provides 1 DOF.
  • Examples: Hydraulic cylinders, drawer slide, telescope extension.

📋 Short Notes

Note  Robot Manipulator

A robot manipulator is a mechanical device that performs tasks by manipulating objects. It is made of a series of links connected by joints, forming a kinematic chain from the base to the end-effector. Used in welding, pick-and-place, painting, and assembly operations. Key parameters: DOF, workspace, payload capacity, and repeatability.

Note  Laws of Robotics

Asimov's three laws: (1) Safety first – robots cannot harm humans, (2) Obedience – robots follow human orders unless it causes harm, (3) Self-preservation – robots protect themselves if it doesn't violate laws 1 or 2. These laws are the foundation of ethical robot design. Modern robotics extends these for AI systems and autonomous vehicles.

Note  Automation in Robotics

Automation refers to using technology to perform tasks without human intervention. In robotics, automation is categorized as: Fixed (one product, high volume), Programmable (reprogrammable, batch production), and Flexible (real-time adaptation). Benefits: Increased speed, consistency, safety, and reduced cost. Challenges: High initial investment, loss of certain jobs, maintenance requirements.

Note  Types of DOF

DOF types: Translational – movement along X, Y, Z axes (3 DOF). Rotational – rotation about X (Roll), Y (Pitch), Z (Yaw) axes (3 DOF). Together they give 6 DOF for full spatial freedom. Redundant robots have 7+ DOF for obstacle avoidance. Serial robots have DOF equal to number of joints. Parallel robots (like Delta robots) have different DOF calculation methods.
